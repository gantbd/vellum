# Structural targets and transformation evidence

Specification §26.3 refines requirements §§3.4.1/5.2.1 at existing IF-01/02/03/05.
Optional providers interpret prose/code/mixed content. The authoritative core
validates declared evidence/dependencies and publishes through the ordinary change
contract; it does not require a parser, schema runtime or complete symbol graph.

## Target and claim obligations

| Value | Required semantic binding | Not established by the value alone |
|---|---|---|
| Structural target | Exact source incarnation/context, provider/profile, relevant region map, interpretation and explicit ordered source spans | Stable symbol identity, permission, schema validity or survival after edits |
| Source claim | Named claim kind, exact source subject, scope/query, applicable standard/dialect/version, provenance, coverage and dependencies | Candidate conformance or global completeness |
| Candidate claim | Exact prepared change/candidate subject and validation scope/profile, plus its source/context dependencies | Permission to publish, truth of undeclared assumptions or successful persistence |
| Lowered proposal | Complete exact changes/expected bytes, write/read/non-text dependencies, required guarantees, resource profile and all actual companions | Semantic correctness merely because source edits are valid |

Provider-local handles select within captured context; stale handles cannot be
looked up against latest text as an implicit retargeting policy. Noncontiguous
targets remain explicit spans, not their bounding interval. Region interpretation
does not change document ownership, and schema/grammar labels cannot load code.
Concrete map/handle/candidate encodings and operation-specific lowering remain gates.

Syntax, schema validity and domain relationships are independent claim kinds.
An operation states its finite required claims; accepted evidence must satisfy
each named claim for its exact subject and declared scope/query. Do not assume
that a binding result substitutes for schema validity, or that syntax establishes
complete reference discovery. Completeness is contextual: unrelated partial
analysis does not invalidate a complete supported claim for a narrower scope.
Matching a declared scope/query still assumes the provider's coverage statement
is truthful; it is not an algorithm proving coverage of arbitrary analysis.
Required claims belong to the accepted operation descriptor and admitted intent;
callers/providers cannot strip them by presenting a shorter list. The private
fixture assumes that trusted capture, rather than proving descriptor lookup or
malformed/dynamic payload decoding. Source-only independence applies to separately
admitted ordinary edits, not reclassification of an existing structural intent.

Candidate validation is outside publication. Its subject is the actual prepared
candidate, including the relevant validation context, not just starting text or
a prior preview. The core's final dependency check must reject evidence invalidated
by changed source, profile, region map, discovery scope or candidate content.
Explicit compatibility may permit reuse only under its supported contract.
No provider callback runs within the serialized decision, and no post-commit
observer can retroactively establish the required precondition.

## Conditional argument and executable checks

Assume sound target mapping, attributable truthful provider evidence, a fixed
supported claim interpretation, correct candidate binding, bounded preparation,
complete declared dependencies and the existing exact-publication suppliers.
For every required claim, admission finds matching evidence for the same candidate,
target, scope/query and applicable current context. Changing one of those relevant
subjects invalidates the match; a different claim kind cannot discharge it. Final
source/dependency/authority checks remain necessary even after evidence matches.
Applying the exact-publication contract then either publishes all actual authored
participants or frames the immediately preceding state on refusal. Evidence adds
preconditions, not a second mutation authority. An operation with no interpretation
claims does not acquire this optional provider dependency.

This is an authored conditional argument, not a new machine-checked theorem,
proof of provider truth or universal semantic correctness. Declared dependencies
do not prove that an agent declared every assumption. Existing exact/release/
normalization/no-op proofs retain their separately stated scopes; no automatic
end-to-end composition/refinement is claimed.

`structural-targets.mjs` checks a private claim-matching reference over all eight
required and eight supplied subsets of three unrelated claim kinds and 16 Boolean
supplier combinations: 1,024 cases. Three literal source fixtures exercise prose,
code and a fenced body through the existing exact-edit reference. They are not
actual Markdown, language or schema implementations. Direct controls check
claim/scope/query mismatch, incomplete required evidence, starting-source versus
candidate validation, altered candidates/maps/read dependencies, context changes,
supplier failures, source-only independence and final permission/source checks.
Input and returned rejected-state frames are checked on the exercised paths.

The private reference uses exact value equality of complete fixture plans/targets,
not a selected wire encoding, hash algorithm, semantic equivalence test or public
schema. Context fields/generations, source fixture limits and supplied Boolean
facts are abstractions. It assumes target mapping and provider attribution/truth,
and passes semantically unchanged companions to the source-only edit reference.
It does not implement region resolution, arbitrary target lowering, conformance
validation, actual declaration completeness, concurrent publication, native fault
containment or recipient projection. Those remain explicit supplier/refinement gates.
Executable evidence records are positive candidate-bound assertions under one
fixed interpretation. They do not model separate source/candidate subject tags,
multiple named schema identities or negative validation verdicts; those belong to
the concrete per-operation claim/report schemas. An actual validation failure
must not be encoded as a matching positive fact. Accepted-descriptor capture and
schema/verdict decoding are supplier gates here, not tested implementations.

## Observation and remaining work

INS-05 records provider/result applicability, required claim class, coverage,
context invalidation and safe failure class; INS-01 records preparation/admission;
INS-02 records actual publication/no-op/refusal; INS-03 governs target/evidence
release. Routing, accepted evidence and committed changes are distinct milestones.
MELT/RED cover bounded work/rates/latency/useful outcomes; USE/golden signals cover
provider demand, retained candidate/evidence occupancy, saturation and pending age.
Missing coverage is unknown, not healthy zero or complete discovery. Diagnostic
capture cannot become an admission authority or alter an already known outcome.

STRIDE targets handle/provider spoofing, candidate substitution, stale-profile
reuse, callback reentry and resource floods. LINDDUN covers targets, standard/schema
locators, source excerpts, correlations and hidden scope/count leakage. STPA/FMEA
targets validation of the wrong candidate, omitted final dependencies and silent
fallback to a weaker command. Use bounded declared classes instead of raw content,
handles, provider errors or arbitrary profile names as metric labels.

The next concrete gates are per-operation supported target/map/claim schemas,
candidate identity, trust/compatibility profiles, complete companion intent and
actual adapter/provider conformance. Generic proof-shaped booleans cannot close
these gates. Required integrations remain required; deferred structural features
are not added by defining the supplied-evidence boundary.
